Privacy Policy

Summary

VANLYFE has no ads and no advertising tracking. We never sell your data, we don't share it with anyone for advertising, and we don't track you across other companies' apps or websites. The app does send some usage and technical data: our own usage ping (which you can turn off), crash reports on iPhone, and the device, usage and crash data that the Google services built into the app collect for their own purposes. Each is described below.

Your location

Your location is used on your phone to find places near you. Your position is shown to other people only if you turn on Show me on the map (Community › Settings › Travelers) or send it in a message (see Messages). With Show me on the map on, the app sends your position to our server, rounded to about 1 km unless you choose Exact, and other travelers who also share can see it with your display name and status for up to 6 hours after your last update. If you also turn on Keep sharing when the app is closed, the app keeps sending it while the app is closed (when you move about 500 m on iPhone, about every 15 minutes on Android). Turning sharing off, signing out or deleting your account removes your position at once. Features that look things up near you send coordinates to the services described below.

Check in nearby uses a foreground location fix only after you choose that action, to suggest mapped places. The fix is held only while the picker is open; only the place you choose is shared when you explicitly post, not the raw fix, accuracy or distance. Choosing a place does not post it. This feature does not enable or change live or background location sharing. You can also check in from a place's details without granting location access.

When you search for a place by name or address, the search goes to Apple's MapKit search service on iPhone, or to Android's built-in Geocoder on Android (on most Android phones, a Google service). When you save an offline map area, the centre of that area goes to the same service to name it.

On Android, if you add the Open nearby home-screen widget and have allowed location access All the time, the widget also gets your location from time to time while the app is closed, to list the closest open places. The widget uses that location only on your phone.

The Today tab shows the weather where you are. To do that, the app sends the coordinates of the area you are looking at (your location, unless you searched somewhere else) to the US National Weather Service (api.weather.gov), rounded to about 10 meters. If you tap Load live map, the same coordinates, rounded to about 100 meters, go to Windy (windy.com). No account details or device identifiers are sent with them.

Air quality, cloud cover and tides send the same coordinates to Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) and NOAA (tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov).

The Pollen card sends the same coordinates, rounded to about 10 km, to Google's Pollen API (Google Privacy Policy).

Nearby articles and local radio on the Today tab send the same coordinates to Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org), rounded to about 100 meters, and to the Radio Browser station directory (radio-browser.info), rounded to about 10 meters. A place's details and the Wikipedia map layer ask Wikipedia for articles around that place or in the map area on screen.

The Crime map layer and a place's Crime nearby section ask our own server for police reports in the map area on screen or around the place you opened. The Transit and LA Metro map layers ask our server for the vehicles in the map area on screen or on the route you pick.

If you turn on Alerts, your phone keeps its last location on the phone only and, about every 30 minutes, sends it to the National Weather Service and Open-Meteo to check for alerts.

Along a route sends your start and destination to Apple Maps (iPhone) or the FOSSGIS OSRM server at routing.openstreetmap.de (Android).

Optional account

You can use every finding feature without an account. If you create one to contribute, we keep your email address, your password and a random user ID on our own Supabase server at vanlife-api.brokejokes.com. If you sign in with Google or Apple instead, we receive only your name and email address from them (Google: the openid, email and profile scopes) and use them only to create and sign in to your VANLYFE account; we never get your password there and never share this data. Passwords are stored hashed, never in plain text. Your email is used only to sign you in, to send sign-in and confirmation codes, and to let members who have your email address in their contacts find your profile (see Finding friends from your contacts). If you turn on Show me on the map, we also keep the display name and status you enter, which other sharing travelers see. If you set up a profile in the Community tab, we also keep the rig, bio and photo you add; every signed-in member can see your profile.

Tips you post are public and show no name.

What you contribute

  • Spots you add and edits you suggest are reviewed before they appear in the public map data.
  • Photos you add are public as soon as they upload, and anyone can report them.
  • Your profile (name, status, rig, bio and photo) is visible to every signed-in member, and any of them can report it.
  • Check-ins share the selected place, optional note and posting time with you and your currently accepted friends only. Current friends can see older check-ins; unfriending or blocking removes access on subsequent reads. Check-ins are self-reported history, not live presence, and stay until you delete them. You can delete your own check-ins, including ones under review.
  • Reporting a check-in hides it from friends and sends a snapshot to moderators. Moderators can access reported check-ins, not browse unreported private posts. Reported snapshots remain for moderation after the post or account is deleted.
  • Reports you send are stored with your user ID when you're signed in.

Messages

Messages you send to other travelers, one to one or in a group, are stored on our server so they can be delivered. They are not end-to-end encrypted. In the app, only the people in the conversation can read them.

In a chat you can also choose Share current location. That sends your exact position once, as a message everyone in the conversation can see, and it is stored like any other message. It is not live sharing: your position isn't updated after that.

When a message arrives, our server sends a notification with the sender's name and the start of the message through Apple Push Notification service (iPhone) or Firebase Cloud Messaging by Google (Android). To do that, the app gives our server a push token for your phone; it is removed when you sign out or delete your account.

If you report a message, it is hidden at once and a copy goes to our moderators, who review it. That copy stays with the report even if the account that sent it is deleted. Our moderators don't read messages nobody reported.

A one-to-one conversation and its messages are deleted when you or the person you wrote to deletes their account. In a group, deleting your account deletes the messages you sent.

Finding friends from your contacts

This is optional and needs an account. If you choose Find friends (Community › Settings › Find friends) and allow access to your contacts, the app reads your contacts on your phone. For each email address in them, it sends only a SHA-256 hash (a one-way fingerprint of the address) to our server, which compares the hashes with members' email addresses and returns the members who match. The hashes are used only to answer that request; our server doesn't store them. Contact names, phone numbers and photos stay on your phone. They are used only to show the list and, if you tap Invite, to start a text message or email that you send yourself. In the same way, a member who has your email address in their contacts can find your profile.

Usage ping

When you open the app, at most once an hour, it sends a short ping to our server: a random install ID that the app makes up on first launch, whether it's the iPhone or Android app, and the app and operating system versions. The ID is not tied to your account or to your device, and deleting the app resets it. Cloudflare, which sits in front of our server, derives an approximate location from your IP address; our server keeps that city-level location (rounded to about 1 km), the country and the time of the ping, and does not store your IP address. We use this only to count how many people use VANLYFE and roughly where. Pings are deleted after 90 days. To stop the ping, turn off Share anonymous usage data in Community › Settings › Privacy & Data.

Crash reports

On iPhone, if the app crashes or runs into an error, it sends a report to our own error-tracking server, which we run ourselves with Bugsink. A report holds what went wrong, the app and iOS versions, the device model, a random ID that the reporting library creates and the app's recent activity, which can include the addresses of its recent web requests and the coordinates in them. The reporting library also sends basic session information: when the app was in use and whether it crashed. Reports don't include your account details. The current Android app doesn't send crash reports to us. Crash and performance reports that Apple's MetricKit hands to the app stay on your iPhone (Community › Settings › About › Diagnostics).

The Google services described below (Google Maps 3D, Places and Firebase) collect their own device, usage and crash data.

Services that receive your device's IP address

When the app loads content, your phone connects directly to these services, which see your device's IP address like any website would:

  • Cloudflare, which fronts our own server at vanlife-api.brokejokes.com (accounts, community, the usage ping, and the crime and transit services) and derives the city-level location described under Usage ping.
  • Our own error-tracking server (iPhone crash reports, described under Crash reports).
  • Apple Push Notification service (iPhone), which delivers message notifications and receives your phone's push token and each notification's sender name and message preview.
  • Firebase Cloud Messaging by Google (Android), which delivers message notifications and receives your phone's push token and each notification's sender name and message preview. Firebase also creates a Firebase installation ID and receives device details and the app version (Google Privacy Policy).
  • Apple MapKit (iPhone) or Android's Geocoder (Android, on most phones a Google service), which receive the places and addresses you search for and the centre of offline map areas you save.
  • GitHub Pages (place data).
  • OpenFreeMap (map tiles).
  • Esri (Satellite and Hybrid map imagery on iPhone), which receives requests for the map area on screen.
  • USGS The National Map (aerial and satellite imagery).
  • Recreation.gov and The Dyrt (place photos).
  • Google Street View, which also receives the place's coordinates (Google Privacy Policy).
  • Google Maps 3D (the 3D view of a place), which also receives the coordinates of the place and of the area you look at in 3D, and collects device and app details, crash reports, an SDK identifier and your map gestures for Google's own use (Google Privacy Policy).
  • Google Maps (Look around and Satellite on place cards, and the map on shared place pages), which also receives the place's coordinates or the Street View panorama next to it (Google Privacy Policy).
  • Mapillary, which also receives the place's coordinates.
  • KartaView and Panoramax (street-level photos), which receive the place's coordinates.
  • Wikimedia Commons (photos nearby and the Photos map layer), which receives the place's coordinates or the edges of the map area on screen.
  • Google Maps Platform (Places: the On Google Maps card and Search Google nearby), which receives the place's name and coordinates, or what you type into Search Google nearby with the coordinates of the map's centre. The Places SDK also collects device and request details, crash reports, an SDK identifier and your IP address for Google's own use (Google Privacy Policy).
  • US National Weather Service (forecasts and alerts) and Windy (live weather map), which receive the coordinates described under Your location.
  • Wikipedia (nearby articles, place details and the Wikipedia map layer) and the Radio Browser station directory (local radio), which receive the coordinates described under Your location.
  • NOAA map services (weather radar and alert map layers), which receive the edges of the map area on screen.
  • National Interagency Fire Center and NASA FIRMS (wildfire data). These are nationwide downloads, and no coordinates are sent.
  • Open-Meteo and NOAA Tides & Currents (air quality, cloud cover and tides), which receive the coordinates described under Your location.
  • Google's Pollen API (the Pollen card), which receives the coordinates described under Your location (Google Privacy Policy).
  • BLM and NASA GIBS: map layer tiles of the area on screen.
  • Other map layers you turn on (such as trails, rivers, earthquakes, road cameras, smoke and air quality) load map tiles of the area on screen or nationwide files from the providers listed in the app under Community › Settings › About › Data sources.
  • FOSSGIS OSRM (Android routes), which receives your start and destination.

Street View, Look around, Satellite, the 3D view, Mapillary, KartaView, Panoramax, Wikimedia Commons, Wikipedia's place and map-layer lookups, the Crime layer, Crime nearby and the On Google Maps card receive the coordinates of the place you are looking at, or of the map area on screen, not your own location. Search Google nearby sends the map's centre, which is your location when the map is centred on you. Links such as Open in Windy or Open in Zoom Earth open in your browser and pass along the coordinates shown on screen.

Deleting your data

In the app, open Community › Settings › Account and tap Delete account. This removes your account, your profile and its photo, your friends list, your check-ins, your one-to-one conversations, the messages you sent, your photos, your push tokens and your traveler profile and position. Reported check-in and message snapshots remain for moderation, with deleted account and post links removed. Spots, edits, tips and other place information you posted stay, without any link to you. Without the app, you can ask by email as described under Delete your account. Clearing the app's storage, or deleting the app, removes everything stored on the phone.
